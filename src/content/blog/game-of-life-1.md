Alright. Day one of the Game of Life. An exercise in discipline, growth, and self-reflection.
In the first of 100 blogposts (hopefully), I will try to explain what this little experiment
of mine is all about, what my motivations are, and how I attempt to tackle this challenge.

## How Game of Life works
Imagine your life as an RPG - that is, a role playing game. Stemming from my love of video games,
I wondered what would happen if I treated my day-to-day experience as a gamified adventure.
That means acquiring experience points, leveling up my skills, tackling quests, among other things.

In layman's terms, each day, I will write a short post about what I did that day. Hopefully, it will
concern an activity that helped me grow, try something new, or create a worthy memory. The minutes or hours
spent on this activity will be quantified into experience points. After much deliberation and considering other
metrics, I decided this to be the most elegant way to measure progress. Since, as we all know, progress comes
in all shapes and sizes, from doing a cool new trick on the first try, to banging your head on the table after failing
it for the thousandth time. Sense of progress and the level of enthusiasm while learning something new may vary,
but the time spent remains objective.

These experience points will then be assigned to a skill track. I outlined six track for now:
- **homesteading**, for doing things around the house (cleaning, home improvements);
- **exploration**, for visiting new places;
- **computers**, for programming, my profession of choice, also IT knowledge, and sure, gaming too I guess;
- **teaching**, for becoming a better teacher (for my highschool compsci teaching gig);
- **chronicling**, for not only writing this blog, but also starting that YouTube channel I've been wanting to since forever;
- and finally, **crafting**, for learning to do things with my hands. Because knowing how to fix stuff is cool as shit.

Each day, I will try to tackle one of these skills, and improve at it. This improvement is measured by the experience gained (or time spent),
and conveyed as levels. Each level requires more experience than the previous one.

I consider the aforementioned topics as growth opportunities. But I also want to instill some routine and discipline.
That's why the second aspect of Game of Life tracks 'dailies'. This one is much simpler.
Each day, I either check off a daily routine quest, or I don't. These will require none or very little learning effort.
The consistency is what counts here. The dailies include:
- sport;
- cooking;
- cleaning;
- reading.

These should be quite self-explanatory. I'm not putting any hard limits on what passes as finished.
Sometimes, I will go for a 1 hour run. Other times, a 20 minute swim. Both count.

I've chosen the arbitrary length of 100 days for the challenge. Because 100 is a round and shiny number.

## Why I'm doing this

Why have I devised an elaborate and seemingly superfluous system to tackle goals that someone might view as ordinary, plain, run-of-the-mill?
Well, because I simply can't get myself to do them. So I have to trick my brain with a bit of gamification.

It's weird. I know I can do hard things. I've done ultra marathons, an ironman, acquired a Master's degree, changed careers sucessfully.
But it seems like it's the little things I can't do well.

Moreover, I've noticed that I more and more feel like a spectator in my own life. Gripped by social media algorithms,
I have become a passive consumer, rather than an active producer. And I detest that.

Exacerbating the situation, I've also become quite the procrastinator. Not much to add on this point.

Instead of attempting a wild 180 degree turn, I will instead try to offset the
direction I've been going in - little by little, but consistently, every day.

I've never done any form of journaling, but I am also curious to see if this habit
can cause some self-reflection and make my mind clearer on the big picture.

I don't actually expect anyone to read this. But that is fine. That's not the point.
The point is to look inward and live more intentionally. So let's see what happens.
