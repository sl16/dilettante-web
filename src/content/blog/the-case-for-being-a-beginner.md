There’s a particular kind of freedom in not knowing what you’re doing yet. You ask obvious questions. You try the wrong thing. You notice what everyone else has learned to ignore.

Somewhere along the way, being a beginner became something to hurry through. I’m trying to resist that.

## Permission to follow a thread

An interest doesn’t need a business plan. A weekend spent learning how typefaces work, or building a tiny website, is valuable even if nothing comes of it besides a better question.

The pressure to specialize can make curiosity feel like a distraction. But the most interesting ideas often turn up between categories.

> You don’t have to become someone else to try something new.

## A small practice

Here’s what I’m trying:

- Pick something because it’s interesting, not because it’s useful.
- Make something small enough to finish.
- Write down what surprised me.
- Leave room to change direction.

None of this requires being particularly good. It only requires paying attention.

This journal is part of that practice. Less of a finished portfolio, more of an open notebook.
